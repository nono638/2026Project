Experiment 2: Chunking × Model Size

Headline findings · 95% bootstrap CIs on the means

Fixed clearly beats sentence; other chunkers tie — and 4B > 9B is real here

Fixed beats sentence; fixed / recursive / semantic are a tie

Mean quality by chunker, with 95% CIs: fixed 4.318 [4.26, 4.38], semantic 4.268 [4.21, 4.33], recursive 4.228 [4.16, 4.29], sentence 4.113 [4.04, 4.18]. Fixed vs sentence is the only chunker pair whose CIs don’t overlap — a real ~0.20 effect. Fixed / recursive / semantic overlap heavily and are indistinguishable. So “chunker barely matters” isn’t quite right; better framing: sentence chunking is worse, the other three are interchangeable.

Model size matters more than chunker, and 4B > 9B is real here

Per-model CIs: qwen3.5:4B (4.577 [4.53, 4.62]) does not overlap qwen3.5:9B (4.446 [4.40, 4.49]). Unlike Experiment 1, where the same comparison was within noise, here the 4B win is statistically supported — likely because Exp 2 holds strategy constant at NaiveRAG and so the only variation is the model itself. 0.8B (3.79) is well below everything else; 2B (4.12) and 4B/9B form two cleanly separated tiers above it.

The winner: fixed-size chunking on 4B

Best config: fixed-size chunker × qwen3.5:4B at 4.649 [4.55, 4.73] mean quality · 74% exact match · 0.27 mean gold F1. Plain fixed-size chunking outperforms the more elaborate sentence-aware approach at every model size, and ties the other two chunkers within CI.

Same reproducibility caveat as Experiment 1

This run also used Ollama’s default temperature ≈ 0.8 with no seed. The CIs above quantify sampling uncertainty over questions; they don’t separately account for stochastic-generation variance. A temperature = 0 + fixed-seed regime would shrink the noise further. See Writeup » Stochastic generation.

Major methodological caveat · read before interpreting any chunker comparison below

The embedder truncated two of the four chunkers’ output

Experiment 2 ran against mxbai-embed-large, which has a 512-token context window. Two of the four chunkers routinely produce chunks larger than that:

  • FixedSizeChunker(500 words) ≈ 700+ tokens — every chunk silently truncated to 512 tokens before embedding (~30% of content discarded).
  • SemanticChunker — variable-size, many chunks truncated.
  • RecursiveChunker(500 chars / 100 overlap) ≈ 100 tokens — safely under the limit, no truncation.
  • SentenceChunker — usually small, no truncation.

So the chunker rankings above describe behaviour under client-side truncation, not the chunkers as designed. Two consequences worth being clear about:

  • The “Fixed beats sentence” finding is conservative — fixed is being penalised by truncation, so the true gap is at least as large as observed, possibly larger.
  • The “Fixed / recursive / semantic are a tie” finding is tainted — we’re comparing (truncated fixed) ≈ (untruncated recursive) ≈ (truncated semantic). The untruncated ranking is genuinely unknown.

This was the reason the project-wide embedder was switched to embeddinggemma:300m (2K-token window) on 2026-05-13 — but that switch happened after Experiment 2 was run, and Experiment 2 has not been re-run. So this dashboard reports what was measured under the truncated regime; an un-truncated re-run is on the Phase 2 backlog. See the Methodology page ’Embedder’ section for the full history.

What This Experiment Tests

Does how you split documents into chunks affect answer quality, and does it interact with model size? A two-axis factorial design: 4 chunking strategies × 4 Qwen 3.5 models = 16 configurations × 200 HotpotQA questions = 3,200 scored answers. RAG strategy is held constant at NaiveRAG to isolate the chunking variable from the strategy effects measured in Experiment 1.

This is an understudied question — most RAG research treats chunking as a fixed preprocessing step. Here we test whether it deserves the same attention as strategy and model selection.

Per-Chunker Ranking (with CIs)

What this shows: Mean quality per chunker across all models and questions, with 95% bootstrap CIs.

How to read it: If two bars’ error intervals overlap, the means aren’t significantly different at the 95% level. Fixed (top) and sentence (bottom) don’t overlap — that’s a real chunker effect. Fixed, recursive, and semantic overlap heavily — on this dataset the three of them are a tie.

Per-Model Ranking (with CIs)

What this shows: Mean quality per model with 95% bootstrap CIs, ordered by parameter count.

How to read it: Unlike Experiment 1, here qwen3.5:4B and qwen3.5:9B do not overlap — the 4B advantage in Exp 2 (where the strategy is held constant at NaiveRAG) is statistically supported, while the same comparison in Exp 1 (varying strategy) was within noise.

Quality Heatmap

What this shows: Mean quality score for every chunker-model combination. Rows are chunking strategies, columns are Qwen3 models ordered by parameter count. Brighter cells = higher quality.

How to read it: Look for whether chunker choice matters at all — if all rows look the same, chunking doesn't matter much. If one row is consistently brighter, that chunker is the clear winner.

Latency Heatmap

What this shows: Mean strategy latency for each configuration. Chunking itself is fast, but different chunk sizes affect retrieval and generation time.

How to read it: Semantic chunking may be slower (requires embedding each chunk boundary). If its latency is high but quality is only marginally better, it's not worth the cost.

Quality vs Model Size

What this shows: Each line is one chunking strategy. X-axis is model size, y-axis is mean quality with error bars.

How to read it: If the lines are nearly parallel, chunking choice doesn't interact with model size — the same chunker wins everywhere. If lines cross, the optimal chunker depends on model size, which is a more interesting finding.

Latency vs Model Size

What this shows: Latency by chunker and model size (log scale).

How to read it: Since all configs use NaiveRAG (same strategy), latency differences come from chunk count affecting retrieval and context length affecting generation time.

Chunking Impact Analysis

What this shows: For each model, the quality gap between the best and worst chunker. Labels show which chunker won and which lost.

How to read it: Tall bars mean chunking choice matters a lot for that model. If bars are short across all models, chunking is a minor variable — effort is better spent on strategy or model selection.

Per-Metric Breakdown

What this shows: Quality decomposed into faithfulness, relevance, and conciseness for all 16 configurations, ordered by overall quality.

How to read it: Does chunking primarily affect faithfulness (getting the right context) or conciseness (answer verbosity)? Relevance should be relatively stable if questions are well-formed.

Score Distributions by Chunker

What this shows: Violin plots of quality for each chunker across all models and questions.

How to read it: A chunker with a narrow, high violin is both good and reliable. Wide spread means inconsistent — it helps some queries but hurts others.

Score Distributions by Model

What this shows: Violin plots grouped by model. Since strategy is held constant (NaiveRAG), this isolates the pure effect of model size.

How to read it: Compare the shapes, not just the means. Does a larger model reduce the "tail" of bad answers, or does it just shift the whole distribution up?

Gold Metrics Heatmap

What this shows: Gold F1 for each configuration — the objective correctness measure independent of the LLM judge.

How to read it: Same pattern as quality heatmap? Good — the judge agrees with ground truth. Different pattern? Investigate why.

Quality vs Latency (Pareto)

What this shows: Each dot is one chunker-model config. The Pareto frontier connects configs where no other is both faster and better.

How to read it: Configs below the frontier are dominated. With only 16 configs, the frontier shape reveals whether bigger chunks (fewer, faster) or smaller chunks (more, better retrieval) win the speed/quality tradeoff.

Chunk Count Analysis

What this shows: Mean number of chunks produced vs. mean quality, colored by chunker. Models within each chunker appear as separate points.

How to read it: Is there an optimal chunk count? Too few means important context is missed. Too many means the model drowns in irrelevant text. The "sweet spot" is where the quality peaks.
